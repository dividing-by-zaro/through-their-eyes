How Cells Get Energy

Every living thing needs energy to survive. Cells get this energy through a process called cellular respiration.

Cellular respiration happens inside tiny structures called mitochondria, often called the "powerhouse" of the cell. During this process, cells break down glucose (a type of sugar) and combine it with oxygen. This chemical reaction releases energy that the cell can use.

The equation for cellular respiration is: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

This is essentially the opposite of photosynthesis, which plants use to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight.

When you breathe in, your lungs take in oxygen and deliver it to your blood. Your blood carries the oxygen to every cell in your body. When cells use the oxygen during respiration, they produce carbon dioxide as waste. Your blood carries this carbon dioxide back to your lungs, where you breathe it out.

This is why you breathe faster when you exercise—your muscles need more energy, so your cells need more oxygen to perform cellular respiration.
