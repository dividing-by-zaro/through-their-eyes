The Industrial Revolution

In the late 1700s, life began to change dramatically in Britain and soon spread across Europe and America. This period of rapid change is called the Industrial Revolution.

Before this time, most people lived in small villages and worked on farms. Goods like clothing and tools were made by hand in homes or small workshops. Everything took a long time to produce.

Then inventors created new machines powered by water and steam. Factories were built near rivers and coal mines. Workers left their farms and moved to cities to find jobs in these factories. Cities grew larger than ever before.

The changes brought both progress and problems. Machines could produce goods faster and cheaper than ever. But factory workers often faced dangerous conditions, long hours, and low pay. Children as young as five worked in mines and mills. Pollution filled the air and water.

Over time, laws were passed to protect workers and improve conditions. The Industrial Revolution transformed society and set the stage for our modern world.
