The Water Cycle

Water is always moving around our planet in a process called the water cycle. This cycle has no beginning or end—it just keeps going.

The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. When water gets warm enough, it evaporates and turns into water vapor, an invisible gas that rises into the air. As the vapor goes higher, it cools down and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds.

When clouds collect enough water droplets, the water falls back to Earth as precipitation—rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some of this water flows into streams and rivers, eventually reaching the ocean. Some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater, which plants use or which slowly moves underground back toward the ocean.

The water cycle is essential for all life on Earth. It provides fresh water for drinking, agriculture, and industry. Without it, our planet would be a very different place.
